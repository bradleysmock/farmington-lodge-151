History of Farmington Masonic Lodge

Farmington Masonic Temple, built in 1876

The Farmington Masonic Lodge has been an integral part of the Farmington community since 1876. The historic building, which exemplifies Victorian and French Renaissance architecture, was constructed for $4,300 and served the dual purpose of housing both the Masonic hall and the township hall1.

This architectural gem stands as one of Oakland County's most attractive historical buildings, featuring distinctive Masonic symbols displayed around the top of the red brick structure. Throughout its history, the building has maintained its role as both a lodge and community gathering place2.

Today, the Farmington Masonic Lodge continues its legacy of fellowship, charity, and community service, upholding the time-honored traditions of Freemasonry while adapting to serve the needs of modern society. The building remains an important landmark in downtown Farmington, connecting the past to the present through its preserved architecture and continued community service.
